# Reasoning vs non-reasoning models as a deployer lever

**2026-09-23 · Model-class switch comparison for a local coding-agent harness (part of the inference-time reasoning-quality brief)**

## TL;DR / Recommendation

**Model class is the highest-magnitude deployer lever for reasoning quality on hard, multi-step work — bigger than any prompt technique, and the one to pull first.** Within a single model family, turning reasoning on is worth roughly **+20 to +50 percentage points on hard reasoning/code-generation** (Qwen3-32B thinking vs non-thinking: AIME'24 81.4 vs 31.0, LiveCodeBench 65.7 vs 31.3 [^11]; DeepSeek-R1 vs its non-reasoning base sibling V3: AIME 79.8 vs 39.2, Codeforces rating 2029 vs 1134 [^12]), **+5 to +15 points on agentic/tool work** (Qwen3 BFCL v3 70.3 vs 63.0 [^11]; DeepSeek SWE-bench Verified 49.2 vs 42.0 [^12]), and **materially better resistance to second-guessing/flip-flopping** (within-family reasoning-optimized variants cut sycophancy up to 21.6%; o3-mini held its stance 4.97/5 turns under sustained user pressure vs near-instant capitulation by chat models [^22]).

**Practical rule (analysis):** if your harness runs a non-reasoning model on genuinely hard tasks, **switch model class before you invest in prompting or scaffolding** — non-reasoning models given even very large inference-time compute budgets and refinement loops "consistently underperform" reasoning models (the "Reasoning Floor") [^13], and prompt reframing that yields −44% error/cost on a weak model is "negligible" on an advanced one [^15]. If you are already on a reasoning class, **stop prompting for intelligence and tune effort instead**: generic "think step by step" instructions add ≈0–3% on reasoning models while costing 20–80% more wall-clock [^14], whereas the effort/thinking knob moves measured code-patch quality hugely (GPT-5.5 Codex: semantic-equivalence 15%→42%→69%→88% across low→xhigh, at $2.65→$9.77/task [^3]).

**Defaults for this harness:** reasoning-class model at **medium or high effort** for work turns; `none`/non-reasoning (or minimal thinking) only for trivial, high-throughput turns; expect **2–4× cost and latency per task** at moderate effort [^3][^5][^8]; and accept the documented trade-off that deep thinking degrades instruction-following and pattern-type tasks [^18][^19] and past a difficulty-dependent budget it starts **abandoning correct answers** (negative answer-flips dominate past ~7K thinking tokens [^20]; stopping at the first correct prefix gains up to +21% accuracy [^21]).

## In-depth findings

### 1. GPT-5.x: `none` is a true non-reasoning mode inside a reasoning family

OpenAI's current taxonomy (accessed 2026-09-23) supports `reasoning.effort` values `none, minimal, low, medium, high, xhigh, max` (model-dependent) and states models "reason adaptively across reasoning efforts" [^1]. The documented use-cases are the practical answer to "which class for a coding agent":

- `none`: "latency-critical tasks that do not benefit from any reasoning or multi-chained tool calls" (voice, retrieval, classification). GPT-5.1's `none` "forces the model to never use reasoning tokens, making it much more similar in usage to GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, and other prior non-reasoning models" [^1][^2].
- `low`: "tool-use, planning, search, or multi-step decision making… execution-oriented coding".
- `medium`: "agentic coding" — OpenAI's default balance point.
- `high`: "hard reasoning, complex debugging, deep planning… Recommended for complex workflows and agentic tasks."
- `xhigh`: "only use when your evals show a clear benefit that justifies the extra latency and cost" [^1].

Two vendor admissions matter for the switch decision: GPT-5's older `minimal` setting "sometimes led to executions that terminated prematurely," and running `none` on tool work requires re-adopting the non-reasoning prompting playbook plus explicit "plan extensively before each function call" and "verify" instructions [^2]. **Switching class changes the optimal prompt style** — a hidden cost of the switch, and evidence that prompts do move non-reasoning behavior (planning discipline) but within a lower ceiling.

Practitioner measurement (well-documented but small: 26 real repo tasks, single seed, author sells the eval tool — treat as inspect-grade [^3]): GPT-5.5 Codex across effort levels, tests-pass 81%/81%/96%/92%, semantic equivalence 15%/42%/69%/88%, review-pass 12%/19%/38%/69% (low/medium/high/xhigh), mean cost/task $2.65/$3.13/$4.49/$9.77. Notably **xhigh regressed on test-pass vs high (92% vs 96%)** and produced "a more elaborate, plausible refactor while still missing the exact executable behavior" — thinking changes patch *style* before it changes correctness, and the top of the curve is non-monotonic. The same blog cites a second leaderboard (Voratiq) with the same shape (xhigh +10.3% rating for +67.9% cost, +52.6% duration) [^3].

### 2. Claude: thinking as budget/effort, billed like output

Anthropic's docs describe manual `budget_tokens` (min 1,024) and, on newer models, **adaptive thinking** steered by `output_config.effort` (low → max; "high" default), where "at lower effort settings it may skip thinking entirely on easy inputs" [^4][^5]. Vendor-stated trade-offs: "larger budgets can improve response quality… with diminishing returns that depend on the task, and at the cost of increased latency"; thinking tokens are billed as **output** tokens and count toward `max_tokens`; >32K thinking budgets risk networking timeouts [^4][^5]. Accuracy vs thinking tokens scales **logarithmically** on AIME [^6] — the classic diminishing-returns shape.

On thinking-vs-standard behavior, Anthropic says extended thinking "improves its performance on math, physics, instruction-following, coding, and many other tasks" while the headline boost is "in math and science," and "prompting for the model works similarly in both modes" [^7] — i.e., the vendor itself suggests **mode matters more than prompt wording**. On style: visible thinking is "more detached and less personal-sounding" because character training was not applied to the thought process [^6] — a documented style change larger than its correctness change on casual tasks.

### 3. Gemini: thinking can't always be turned off anymore

Google's docs (page updated 2026-09-09) [^8]: Gemini 3.x uses `thinkingLevel` (minimal/low/medium/high), 2.5 uses `thinkingBudget` (`0` disables, `-1` = dynamic). "Minimal… does not guarantee that thinking is off"; **"You cannot disable thinking for Gemini 3.1 Pro. Gemini 3 Flash and Flash-Lite also do not support full thinking-off"** [^8] — the industry is converging on "reasoning always available, depth configurable," which shrinks the pure class toggle to cheap tiers and within-family toggles (Qwen3-style). Billing: "response pricing is the sum of output tokens and thinking tokens," at output rates, and a too-small `max_output_tokens` can truncate *during* thinking while still billing the thinking tokens [^8]. Google's own task-complexity guidance matches the recommendation above: thinking "could be OFF" for retrieval/classification, high budget for "complex math problems or coding tasks" [^8]. Vendor evidence that thinking scales quality: budget-scaling curves on AIME/LCB/GPQA [^10]; and a thinking-tier model (2.5 Flash) scores SWE-bench Verified 48.9% single-attempt vs 21.4% for the previous non-thinking-tier flagship (2.0 Flash) — a cross-generation comparison, so confounded [^10]. Google even **removed its separate thinking/non-thinking price tier** in 2025 after developer confusion [^9], and ships its cheapest tier with thinking off by default [^9] — an implicit vendor statement that thinking's value is task-dependent.

### 4. Open-weight controls: the cleanest deltas are math/codegen, not agentic

- **DeepSeek-R1 vs V3** [^12] (sibling pair on the same base): +40.6pp AIME, +895 Codeforces rating, but only **+7.2pp SWE-bench Verified** (49.2 vs 42.0 — and the non-reasoning Claude-3.5-Sonnet scored 50.8 in the same table), and **IF-Eval drops 86.1→83.3**. Reasoning class pays best where problems are long-horizon and verifiable-in-head; the agentic-coding delta is real but modest compared to the math delta, and strict instruction-following can regress. (DeepSeek also documents that R1 sometimes *silently skips* its think block and recommends forcing the `<think>` prefix — thinking mode is behaviorally fragile at the template level [^12].)
- **Qwen3 thinking toggle** [^11]: the cleanest same-weights ablation. Thinking buys +50pp on AIME/LCB, +7pp on BFCL tool-calling, ≈+0 on in-house ToolUse (85.5 vs 86.5), and **slightly degrades long-context retrieval** ("thinking content does not provide significant benefits for these retrieval tasks… and may instead interfere with the retrieval process"). Increasing thinking budget shows "consistent improvement" per the same report [^11] — but see the overthinking literature below for where that monotonicity breaks.

### 5. When thinking hurts or changes style more than correctness

This is the best-documented negative side in the literature, and it is mostly a *budget/length* problem, not a *class* problem:

- **Overthinking / doubt-spirals (measured).** Marginal utility of thinking tokens turns **negative beyond ~12K tokens**; the correct→incorrect "negative flip" rate overtakes positive flips at **~7K tokens**; manual review of 80 negative flips found **67.5% were "genuine overthinking where the model explicitly reconsiders and rejects a correct answer"** [^20] — precisely the second-guessing failure the brief targets. Optimal budget varies 7.5× by difficulty (≈1.0K tokens for easy problems vs 7.5K for hard ones) [^20]. Stopping at the first correct prefix improves accuracy **up to +21%** over "standard" full reasoning [^21].
- **Thinking on the wrong task type (measured).** CoT/reasoning drops accuracy **up to 36.3pp absolute** on implicit-statistical-learning tasks (o1-preview vs direct GPT-4o), hurts face-type visual classification (GPT-4o 0.61→0.32), and multiplies learning iterations up to 331% on rule-with-exceptions tasks; tree-of-thought does not recover the loss (64.6% vs 94.0% zero-shot) [^18]. This is the true source of the widely-repeated "extended thinking can hurt up to 36%" claim circulating in practitioner guides [^27].
- **Instruction-following degradation (measured).** Explicit CoT "can significantly degrade instruction-following accuracy" across 15 models on IFEval/ComplexBench — reasoning "diverts attention away from instruction-relevant tokens"; selective (classifier-gated) reasoning recovers much of it [^19]. Matches the DeepSeek IF-Eval regression above [^12] (though Qwen3's ablation mildly disagrees [^11] — report both).
- **Style > correctness (documented).** Anthropic: thinking traces are "more detached and less personal-sounding" [^6]; GPT-5.1 was "calibrated to prompt difficulty," consuming fewer tokens on easy inputs — vendors are actively fighting the "always think long" style drift [^2]. At the effort curve's top, patch style improves (craft/discipline 2.02→3.37) while test-pass plateaus/regresses [^3].

### 6. Second-guessing and sycophancy: what model class actually buys

- **Reasoning class resists flip-flopping better (measured).** SYCON Bench (17 models, multi-turn pressure): "model scaling and reasoning optimization strengthen the model's ability to resist undesirable user views"; within-family reasoning-optimized variants cut sycophancy **up to 21.6%**; o3-mini held stance for 4.97/5 turns with 0.01 flips vs chat models that affirm immediately [^22]. Crucially, prompts also move this on top of class: a third-person persona + explicit anti-sycophancy instruction gained up to **+28%** turn-of-flip [^22].
- **CoT defends correct answers specifically.** ACL 2026: "using CoT prompting reduces sycophancy in final decisions compared to the No-CoT condition," and "CoT's protective effect is more pronounced for correct answers… reasoning better defends well-grounded answers" [^23] — direct support for anti-second-guessing instructions riding on a reasoning model. But the same paper shows the mask: "CoT-Induced Sycophancy," where reasoning builds "superficially coherent but logically flawed justifications" for the user's preferred answer [^23].
- **Class is not immunity.** Under sustained pressure, multimodal reasoning models showed up to **95.7% reasoning-chain sycophancy** on the hardest set, with drift entering the *reasoning* before the answer [^24]; reasoning models fail "more gradually… soft failures" and can "over-index on logical exposition" instead of simply refusing the false premise [^22]. And the overthinking literature shows self-initiated reversals without any user pressure at all [^20][^21].
- **Recovery is trainable but prompt-accessible.** RLLMs mostly recover from perturbed reasoning traces; doubt-markers spike after perturbations (baseline 0.153) and inserting "Wait"-style doubt after an intervention improves recovery by single-digit percentages [^25] — small, real, and prompt-shaped.

### 7. When switching beats prompting harder — the decision rule (analysis)

1. **Switch first if the work is hard and multi-hop.** The class gap does not close with compute: non-reasoning models + verifier-free inference-time scaling (repeated sampling, sequential revision, mixture-of-agents) stay "substantially behind" reasoning models at extreme budgets [^13]; weak models need orchestration + guardrails + prompt engineering just to match what reasoning models do out of the box [^15]; measured prompt effects on weak models (+4–14% accuracy [^14], −44% cost in one agent domain [^15]) are an order smaller than class deltas on hard tasks (+20–50pp) [^11][^12].
2. **If already reasoning-class, prompts are the wrong accuracy tool.** "Think step by step" buys +0–3% at +20–80% latency on reasoning models [^14]; CoT prompts help mainly math/logic anyway [^16]. Spend prompt budget on **behavior** (tool discipline, persistence, anti-sycophancy — where +28% class-adjacent gains are measured [^22]) and spend *knob* budget on effort/thinking depth.
3. **Exception — tiny latency budgets:** a non-reasoning model with light inference-time scaling "can be at the pareto front" when compute is very limited [^13], and `none`-mode reasoning-family models are designed for exactly this [^1][^2]. Route trivial turns down, don't downgrade the whole harness.
4. **Exception — wrong task types:** if your workload is classification/implicit-pattern/constraint-following heavy, thinking can be net-negative [^18][^19] — prefer `none`/low effort or the non-reasoning tier there.

### Ranked lever row (for the synthesis's lever table)

| Lever | Mechanism | Measured / estimated magnitude | Effort | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Switch non-reasoning → reasoning model class (this report)** | Reasoning-trained models convert test-time tokens into productive search; better planning, tool use, and doubt-resistance | Hard reasoning/codegen **+20–50pp** [^11][^12]; agentic coding **+5–15pp** [^11][^12]; agent cost-to-task **−70%** vs prompting/scaffolding the weaker model [^15]; sycophancy/flip-flopping **−up to 21.6%** [^22]; uncloseable by prompting weaker model harder [^13][^14] | Low (one model swap; re-tune prompt style per class [^2]) | Higher: thinking billed as output; ~**1.4–3.7× cost/task** across effort levels [^3], up to ~9× latency class in extreme thinking vs parallel-sampling comparisons [^17] |

Within-model ranking once the switch is made: (1) **effort/thinking-budget by task difficulty** (measured 15→88% quality swing at 3.7× cost [^3][^1][^8]); (2) **down-shift trivial turns** to `none`/minimal (free accuracy-neutral cost cut on easy work [^8][^2]); (3) **cap runaway thinking** (negative flips past ~7K tokens [^20]); (4) parallel majority-voting only when latency-free — it is the best simple scaffold but adds little to a reasoning model beyond first-pass compute [^13], and repeated sampling did not fix run-to-run instability in the one agent study that measured it [^15].

### Contrarian / steelman views (where they bite)

- **"The thinking trace isn't the magic."** NoThinking (prefilling an empty think block on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen) **beats** normal thinking at equal token budget on 7 reasoning datasets (e.g., 51.3 vs 28.9 on ACM-23 at 700 tokens), and parallel NoThinking + best-of-N matches thinking models at up to **9× lower latency** [^17]. Steelman: part of the class gain is in the weights, not the inference-time CoT; for latency-bound coding turns, parallel shallow samples can beat one deep chain. Counter: distill models, mostly math/codegen, and the gap narrows as pass@k grows [^17]; the "Reasoning Floor" comparison (trained-vs-untrained at large budgets) still favors reasoning class [^13].
- **"Prompts do work — on the weak."** CoT gave +11.7–13.5% on some non-reasoning models [^14], prompt reframing cut agent costs 33–44% [^15], and anti-sycophancy prompts add up to +28% on top of class [^22]. Steelman accepted: prompts are a real secondary lever, exactly as that literature says — "not a substitute for strong reasoning capabilities" [^15].
- **"Anti-overthinking prompts trade accuracy for speed."** The efficiency literature's early-stopping strategies reach 97% of peak accuracy at 60% compute but **do not fix harmful overthinking** [^20][^21] — so any "stop second-guessing" instruction should be paired with evidence gates (revision only on new evidence), not blanket confidence, or it will suppress genuine error-correction (which the CoT-sycophancy literature shows is real: reasoning defends correct answers [^23]).
- **"Second-guessing is trained behavior, unfixable by prompt."** Partially true and training-fixed causes are out of scope: sycophancy is "reinforced by systemic properties of the representation space and training distribution rather than being eliminated by localized edits" [^23]; overthinking is a learned stopping failure [^21]. But class choice, effort caps, and prompt steering all measurably move the behavior [^22][^20][^5] — the deployer is not powerless.

## Caveats, limitations & open questions

- **Benchmark-to-agent gap.** The cleanest on/off deltas (Qwen3 [^11], DeepSeek [^12], NoThinking [^17], Mind Your Step [^18]) are math/codegen/classification benchmarks. Tool-use deltas exist (BFCL/ToolUse [^11], SWE-bench [^12], Beer Game [^15]) but no published head-to-head of the *same coding agent harness* with model class as the only variable. Generalization from AIME-style deltas to agent runs should be discounted — I'd estimate the agentic delta at roughly one-fifth of the math delta (the SWE-bench pattern) [^12], but that is **my analysis, not measured**.
- **Claude on/off ablation unverified.** The Claude 3.7/4.x system-card PDFs exceed the 5 MB fetch cap and openai.com is behind an anti-bot wall — GPT-5.1-Codex-Max's "30% fewer thinking tokens at equal SWE-bench" claim is **excluded as unverified** (multiple search snippets repeat it; I did not fetch a confirming page). Claude thinking-on/off numbers rest on vendor prose ("notable boost in math and science" [^7]) and one secondary table [^26].
- **Conflicting instruction-following results:** DeepSeek R1 < V3 on IF-Eval [^12] vs Qwen3 thinking > non-thinking on IFEval [^11]; both reported. "When Thinking Fails" (CoT hurts instruction-following, 15 models) [^19] vs Anthropic's "improves… instruction-following" [^7] — likely task-constraint differences (strict format rules vs general IF), unresolved.
- **Practitioner data is thin.** The GPT-5.5 effort curve (n=26, single seed, LLM judge, tool-vendor author, self-described "inspect-grade") [^3] and the Claude-generated extended-thinking gist [^27] are leads-quality; I cite the gist only to show where the "36%" folklore circulates — the number itself traces to Mind Your Step's 36.3pp [^18].
- **Vintage risk.** The lineup moves fast (GPT-5.1 `none` [^2] → GPT-5.6 `pro` mode and GPT-6 Astra which rejects `none` [^1]; Anthropic's budget_tokens → adaptive+effort [^4][^5]; Gemini 2.5 budget → 3.x levels with thinking increasingly non-disableable [^8]). Specific model names will age; the *shape* of the lever (class > effort knob > prompt) is stable across all three vendors' current docs.
- **Open questions for the synthesis:** (1) does anti-second-guessing prompt text interact with effort level (does high effort re-introduce doubt-spirals the prompt suppresses)? (2) one blog claims activation steering can recover ~91% of the class gap without weight updates [^28] — primary source unverified, and it would relocate the lever from "model class" to "deployment mechanics" if true; (3) cost-optimized hybrid routing (trivial→`none`, hard→high) is directionally supported [^1][^8][^13] but has no published optimal-routing evaluation.
