# Runtime knobs: reasoning effort, thinking budget, sampling, context hygiene

**2026-09-23 · Per-run runtime controls for a tool-using coding agent (OpenAI / Anthropic / Google), measured behavior & recommended settings**

## TL;DR / Recommendation

**Set effort explicitly to `medium`-equivalent for routine agentic coding and `high`/`xhigh` only for genuinely hard steps; never starve the output cap (thinking tokens live inside it); and spend most of your engineering effort on context hygiene — it moves accuracy more than any thinking knob.** Specifically, for an agentic coding harness:

1. **Effort/thinking level (biggest single knob):** run at `medium` (OpenAI `reasoning.effort`, Gemini `thinking_level`) or `high`/`xhigh` (Claude `output_config.effort`) by default; **bump to `high`/`xhigh`/`max` per hard step, not globally**. Measured gains are large at the bottom of the scale (GPT-5 `minimal`→`low` moved an intelligence index 44→64) and flatten above `medium` on bounded tasks (GPT-5 scored identical 97% at `medium` and `high` on basic math while burning more tokens); on multi-file refactors `high` has actually regressed vs `medium` via over-engineering [^25][^19][^26]. All three providers now say: treat effort as a tuning knob, not the fix for quality drops [^1][^8][^15].
2. **Token ceilings (cheap insurance, catastrophic if wrong):** thinking tokens are billed and capped as *output*. Truncating a reasoning model mid-think collapses accuracy (72.2%→53.5% at a 1,024-token cap in one study) and produces empty responses you pay for [^19][^1][^7]. Reserve ≥25k tokens for reasoning+output on OpenAI (their number), `max_tokens` ≥64k at Claude `xhigh`/`max`, and never use a small `max_output_tokens` as a cost control on Gemini — lower `thinking_level` instead [^1][^8][^14].
3. **Context hygiene (largest measured magnitude):** models lose >50% of short-context accuracy by 32K on non-literal retrieval, drop 39% on average in multi-turn settings, and degrade from length and distractors alone [^21][^22][^23]. Enable provider compaction/context-editing (OpenAI `context_management.compact_threshold` / `/responses/compact`; Claude `clear_tool_uses_20250919` tool-result clearing), keep stale tool output out of context, and round-trip reasoning items/thinking blocks unmodified [^4][^10][^1][^7].
4. **Sampling knobs are a dead lever on current reasoning models** — temperature/top_p are rejected, error, or (Gemini) formally deprecated as of Aug 2026. Don't design around them [^2][^7][^16].
5. **Against overthinking:** more budget is not free quality. Longer reasoning is *measured* to cause models to abandon previously correct answers (a doubt-spiral in the data), and both Anthropic (`max` effort "can lead to overthinking" on structured-output tasks) and OpenAI ("xhigh… only when your evals show a clear benefit") say the same [^18][^8][^1]. Cut doubt-spirals with *effort caps + context cleanliness*, and preserve genuine error-correction by escalating effort per hard step rather than suppressing thinking globally. Note the counter-evidence: blunt "answer concisely" prompts cut tokens 38–63% but **lose 1.4–3.9 accuracy points** — anti-overthinking prompt clauses can trade accuracy for speed [^19].

Confidence key used below: **[documented]** = official provider docs (fetched 2026-09-23); **[measured]** = empirical study with numbers; **[provider claim]** = vendor assertion without published data; **[practitioner]** = non-peer-reviewed measurement; **[analysis]** = my synthesis.

## In-depth findings

### 1. OpenAI: `reasoning.effort`, mode, context, and the token ceiling

**Names/values/defaults [^1][^2] [documented].** The parameter is `reasoning.effort` (Responses API; use Responses for tool calling — GPT-6 Astra's function calling requires it) or `reasoning_effort` (Chat Completions). Supported values are "model-dependent and can include `none`, `minimal`, `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, and `max`." Defaults are explicitly *not* universal: `gpt-5.5` defaults to `medium`; GPT-5.6 defaults to `medium` in both `standard` and `pro` `reasoning.mode`; GPT-6 Sol and Luna default to `medium`; GPT-6 Astra does not support `none` at all (400). Some models support only a subset — check the model page [^1]. OpenAI's own per-tier guidance maps `low` to "execution-oriented coding," `medium` to "agentic coding," `high` to "agentic coding, complex debugging," `xhigh` to "long runs… only use when your evals show a clear benefit that justifies the extra latency and cost," and `max` to "compare against xhigh first" [^1]. GPT-6 migration guidance says if you were on `minimal`, "start with `low` and compare results" [^2].

**Measured magnitude.** Effort is a real lever but with a steep low end and a flat top. Artificial Analysis swept all four GPT-5 effort levels: intelligence index 44/64/67/68 (minimal/low/medium/high) with a 23× token spread high-vs-minimal, noting "between medium and high there is less of an uplift" [^25] **[measured, Aug 2025]**. On basic arithmetic, LLMThinkBench found GPT-5 at 97% for *both* `medium` and `high` and o3 flat at 97% across all three effort levels with ~8% more tokens at `high` — i.e., on easy tasks effort mostly changes *style and token count, not accuracy* [^19] **[measured]**. On a 60-task multi-file refactor benchmark, `medium` beat `high` (73.1% vs 71.4% on GPT-5.5 Pro) with 23% of high-effort runs producing over-engineered edits that failed integration tests [^26] **[practitioner — single vendor-run study, treat directionally]**. This is consistent with Anthropic documenting that on Claude Opus 5 "effort controls thinking volume, not visible response length: changing effort does not reliably shorten responses" — effort primarily buys thinking depth and tool-call thoroughness, not different answers on easy work [^8].

**When more hurts.** Beyond `medium` on bounded work: plateau or regression [^19][^26]; at `xhigh`/`max`, latency and cost rise steeply while gains are eval-dependent per OpenAI's own wording [^1]. And more tokens in the chain measurably induce answer-flipping: "extended reasoning is associated with abandoning previously correct answers" [^18], with a hand-annotated taxonomy showing self-contradiction loops in 31% (GPT-5) and 34% (o3/o4-mini) of long traces and a documented "self-doubt loop" case ("Wait, reconsider… is 89 largest?") [^19] **[measured]**. Directly relevant to the brief's second-guessing concern: **unbounded effort is a mechanism for self-reversal**, not just waste.

**Mid-run and state knobs [^1][^5] [documented].** `configuration_update` input items change effort mid-conversation on GPT-6 (standard, single-agent), preserving the prompt-cache prefix — the sanctioned way to run routine steps at `low`/`medium` and escalate to `high` for a hard debugging step. `reasoning.context: current_turn` vs `all_turns` trades reasoning continuity for rendered-context size; OpenAI explicitly positions `current_turn` as reducing rendered context on long runs, and `all_turns` (GPT-5.6 default) as increasing billed input [^1][^5]. In tool loops, OpenAI "highly recommends" passing back all reasoning items since the last user message — "the model [can] continue its reasoning process to produce better results in the most token-efficient manner"; using stateless Chat Completions instead yields "slightly degraded model performance and greater reasoning token usage in complex agentic cases involving many function calls" [^1][^3].

### 2. Anthropic: adaptive thinking, `budget_tokens`, effort, `task_budget`

**The landscape has moved to adaptive thinking [^6][^13] [documented].** Manual mode (`thinking: {type:"enabled", budget_tokens:N}`) survives only on older models (Sonnet 4.5/Opus 4.5/Haiku 4.5/earlier). It is **deprecated** on Opus 4.6/Sonnet 4.6 and **returns 400** on Opus 4.7/4.8, Opus 5/5.5, Sonnet 5, Fable 5/5.1, Mythos 5/5.1 — which are "adaptive-only" for Fable/Mythos. Migration: drop `budget_tokens`, set `thinking: {type:"adaptive"}`, and control depth via `output_config: {effort: ...}` (effort must be in `output_config`, *not* inside `thinking`, or 400) [^6][^13]. Anthropic states adaptive thinking "reliably drives better performance than extended thinking with a fixed `budget_tokens`" **[provider claim — no numbers published]** [^13]. On Sonnet 5/Opus 5, omitting `thinking` selects **adaptive thinking**, not off — a migration trap that silently starts billing thinking tokens [^13].

**`budget_tokens` mechanics (legacy but still deployed) [^6] [documented].** Minimum **1,024** (API rejects less); must be **less than `max_tokens`** because thinking counts toward the turn's `max_tokens` — the single exception is interleaved thinking (tool loops), where the budget spans all thinking blocks in one assistant turn. The budget is "a target rather than a strict cap"; `max_tokens` is the hard ceiling. Tuning guidance: start near 1,024 for simple tasks; 16,000+ for complex tasks; diminishing returns with added latency; budgets above 32k belong in batch processing to avoid network timeouts. Changing `budget_tokens` between requests **invalidates prompt-cache breakpoints** (the budget is rendered into the prompt) — "pick a budget and hold it stable for the life of a cached conversation" [^6].

**Effort (current depth control) [^8][^9] [documented].** `output_config.effort` ∈ `low|medium|high|xhigh|max`; default `high` on most models, **`medium` on Opus 5.5**. Effort scales the whole response — text, tool calls, *and* thinking: "Lower effort… combine multiple operations into fewer tool calls… proceed directly to action without preamble," while higher effort "may make more tool calls… explain the plan before taking action… include more comprehensive code comments" [^8]. That is the clearest official statement that effort settings change **style/behavior** as much as raw accuracy. Per-model coding recommendations are strong: Opus 4.7/4.8 — "**start with `xhigh` for coding and agentic use cases**," `max` reserved for frontier problems and explicitly flagged for overthinking on structured output; Opus 5 — `high` default, "step up to `xhigh` for demanding coding and agentic work," and "use `low` and `medium` liberally… wherever your evals show quality holds" [^8]. Anthropic's guidance for a shallow-reasoning complaint is knob-first: "raise effort rather than prompting around it" [^8].

**Truncation and ceilings [^7][^9] [documented].** `max_tokens` is a strict limit covering thinking + response; at `xhigh`/`max` "set a large `max_tokens`… starting at 64k tokens… is a reasonable default" [^8]. If you see `stop_reason: "max_tokens"`: "raise `max_tokens`… or lower the effort level… If quality on those requests matters, raise the cap; if they were over-thought, lower the effort" [^9]. Current-model ceilings: 128K output (Fable 5/5.1, Mythos 5/5.1, Opus 4.6–5.5, Sonnet 5/4.6) and 64K (Opus 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Haiku 4.5); SDKs require streaming above `max_tokens` 21,333 [^7]. Context windows: 1M (Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5), 200K (Haiku 4.5) [^12] **(cost fact, one line: Fable 5.1 $10/$50 per MTok in/out; Opus 5.5 $4/$20)**.

**`task_budget`: the loop-level anti-overrun knob [^11] [documented, beta].** `output_config.task_budget = {type:"tokens", total, remaining?}` (header `task-budgets-2026-03-13`; min `total` 20,000; Opus 4.7+, Fable/Mythos; not Sonnet 5, not Claude Code). The model sees a server-injected countdown across one agentic loop and "prioritize[s] work and finish gracefully as the budget is consumed"; adaptive thinking "scales down as the budget depletes." It is **advisory** — combine with `max_tokens` for a hard cap. Sizing guidance is empirical: measure current per-task spend and start at your p99 [^11]. For the second-guessing problem this is the closest thing to a "commit and finish" runtime control: it bounds the runway for doubt-spirals without suppressing reasoning at the step level. **[analysis: plausible mechanism; no published accuracy effect yet.]**

**Per-message effort & thinking steering [^9] [documented].** Two cache-friendly modulation tools: (a) per-message `output_config` on a system message (beta `mid-conversation-output-config-2026-07-01`; changes effort without breaking cache — top-level changes restart the cache and "stee[r] the model less reliably"); (b) per-message natural-language steering with Anthropic's exact phrases: `"Please think hard before responding."` (encourage) and `"Answer directly without deliberating."` (suppress) — the docs explicitly suggest an "agent harness… append the encouraging phrase on planning steps and the suppressing phrase on routine confirmations." Order of operations is prescribed: "If you want Claude to think less often, lower the effort level before reaching for prompt-based steering," and measure steering changes before shipping [^9]. Anthropic also warns against over-suppressing: "Steering Claude to think less often may reduce quality on tasks that benefit from reasoning" [^13].

### 3. Gemini: `thinkingLevel` / `thinkingBudget` / `max_output_tokens`

**Names/values/defaults [^14][^15] [documented] (thinking page last updated 2026-09-09).** Gemini 3+ uses `thinkingLevel` (`minimal|low|medium|high`); Gemini 2.5 uses `thinkingBudget` (int). They are mutually exclusive per generation; the 3.8 Flash migration checklist says "Replace `thinking_budget` with the string enum `thinking_level`" [^15]. Dynamic thinking (model allocates what it needs) is the default posture; `thinkingBudget = -1` selects it on 2.5 [^14]. Support matrix highlights: 3.8/3.7 Flash default `medium` and **reject `minimal`**; 3.1 Pro defaults `high` (dynamic) and thinking **cannot be disabled**; 3.5/3.1 Flash-Lite default `minimal` ("does not guarantee thinking is off"); 2.5 Pro range 128–32,768 (off = N/A); 2.5 Flash 0–24,576 (`0` off); 2.5 Flash-Lite 512–24,576. The budget "might overflow or underflow" per prompt — a soft guide, not a cap [^14]. For Gemini 3.8 Flash (1M context, 64K output), Google's recommended mapping: `low` for latency-critical work, `medium` (default) "for complex code and agentic use cases… higher first-pass accuracy," `high` "for deep reasoning, mathematics, and difficult multi-step tasks" [^15]. Google also models 3.8 Flash's agentic behavior as "smaller reasoning steps, calls tools iteratively, and verifies its work," noting "not every workflow needs this level of verification" — drop `thinking_level` for routine work [^15].

**`max_output_tokens` interplay [^14] [documented] — the sharpest rule of the three providers:** `max_output_tokens` includes thought tokens and "acts as a hard cutoff enforced by the infrastructure **without changing how the model allocates its thinking budget**." Hitting it while reasoning → `finish_reason: MAX_TOKENS`, truncated or empty output, thinking tokens still billed. "To reduce cost or latency without truncating responses, lower `thinking_level`… instead of setting a small `max_output_tokens`." Google's best-practices also confirm prompt-level thinking control is sanctioned ("provide guidance in your prompt to constrain the amount of thinking… lets you reserve more of the token output for your response") — i.e., the knob and the prompt both steer thinking, and long outputs compete with thinking for the same envelope [^14]. Multi-turn state: return `thought_signatures` unchanged (required with function calling) [^14].

### 4. Temperature / top-p: a dead lever on reasoning models

**[documented, three providers converging]** On current Claude models (Fable 5/5.1, Mythos 5/5.1, Opus 5.5/5/4.8/4.7, Sonnet 5), "non-default `temperature`, `top_p`, or `top_k` values return a 400 error **on every request, regardless of whether thinking is used**"; on older models the restriction applies while thinking is on (`temperature`/`top_k` incompatible; `top_p` allowed 0.95–1) [^7]. For GPT-6 migration OpenAI says: "When reasoning effort is not `none`, remove `temperature`, `top_p`, and `top_logprobs`," and Azure's docs list `temperature, top_p, presence_penalty, frequency_penalty, logprobs, top_logprobs, logit_bias, max_tokens` as unsupported for reasoning models [^2][^5]. *Source tension to note:* Azure's GPT-6 feature table marks `temperature`/`top_p` ✅ for GPT-6 models while its "unsupported parameters" note carves out GPT-6 Astra [^5]; the reconciliation consistent with both sources is that GPT-6 accepts sampling parameters only at `reasoning_effort: "none"`, per OpenAI's migration guidance [^2]. Gemini deprecated `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k` outright — changelog 2026-08-26: "The sampling parameters `temperature`, `top_p` and `top_k` are now deprecated," and the 3.8 Flash checklist says "Strip `temperature`, `top_p`, and `top_k` from generation configs" [^16][^15]. (Whether legacy calls on 3.6 Flash/3.5 Flash-Lite are silently ignored vs. accepted is claimed by third-party blogs but **not verified here** — see Caveats.) **[analysis]** Practical consequence for the harness: don't build variance-diversity or "creativity" workflows on sampling knobs for reasoning models; use effort and parallel sampling elsewhere instead (out of scope here). Chroma's context-rot study itself had to run `temperature=0 "unless that setting is incompatible (i.e. o3) or explicitly discouraged (Qwen's thinking mode)" — independent corroboration that reasoning stacks lock sampling [^23].

### 5. Max-token and context-window management

**[documented]** Common rule across providers: **thinking tokens are output tokens** — they are billed as output (OpenAI: "still occupy space in the model's context window and are billed as output tokens" [^1]; Anthropic: same, plus prior-turn thinking blocks kept in context are billed as *input* on keep-all models [^7]; Gemini: pricing = output + full thought tokens, even though only summaries are returned [^14]).

- **Sizing:** OpenAI: reserve ≥25k tokens for reasoning+output initially, then tune from `usage.output_tokens_details.reasoning_tokens`; handle `status:"incomplete"` / `incomplete_details.reason:"max_output_tokens"` or you'll treat paid empty responses as empty results [^1][^5]. Anthropic: `max_tokens` ≥64k at `xhigh`/`max`; remember each request in a tool loop has its own `max_tokens`, so it bounds a *request*, not the loop — `task_budget` bounds the loop (softly) [^8][^11]. Gemini: don't use `max_output_tokens` as a budget at all — use `thinking_level` [^14].
- **Window sizes (current flagship lines):** GPT-6 family: 1,050,000-token context, 922,000 max input, 128,000 max output (input/output share the budget, not additive) [^5]. Claude Fable 5.1/Opus 5.5/Sonnet 5: 1M context, 128K output; Haiku 4.5: 200K/64K [^12]. Gemini 3.8 Flash: 1M context, 64K output [^15].
- **Long-request mechanics:** Anthropic SDKs require streaming when `max_tokens` > 21,333; >~32k thinking per request → batch to avoid timeouts [^7]. OpenAI recommends reserving headroom because a request can "generate anywhere from a few hundred to tens of thousands of reasoning tokens" [^1].

### 6. Context hygiene: the highest-magnitude deployer lever

**Positional degradation [measured].** "Lost in the Middle" (TACL 2023) established the U-curve: performance peaks when the needed information is at the very start or end and degrades in the middle — GPT-3.5-Turbo lost >20% absolute on multi-document QA and in the worst case scored *below its closed-book baseline (56.1%)* when the answer sat mid-context; GPT-4 shows the same U-shape at higher absolute accuracy; instruction fine-tuning narrowed but did not remove the gap [^20]. **Recency caveat:** this is 2023-vintage models, and Chroma's 2025 study, using a non-lexical NIAH variant on 18 newer models, found "no notable variation in performance" across 11 needle positions [^23] — i.e., mid-context effects are task-dependent (literal QA vs. their variant), not universal. Both results stand: position matters most when retrieval requires reasoning, which is exactly what agent transcripts are.

**Length/distractor decay [measured].** NoLiMa (ICML 2025): of 13 models nominally supporting ≥128K, at 32K "11 models drop below 50% of their strong short-length baselines"; GPT-4o fell 99.3%→69.7%; "even models enhanced with reasoning capabilities or CoT prompting struggle" [^21]. Context Rot (18 models): with task difficulty held constant, performance degrades with input length alone; a single distractor measurably hurts and four compound; the harm from distractors *amplifies* as length grows; in LongMemEval, all models scored markedly better on focused (~300-token) than full (~113k-token) inputs, and thinking modes helped both without closing the gap [^23]. Notably for the anti-second-guessing theme: on full-context LongMemEval prompts, Claude models failed by **over-abstaining** — "the specific dates for these events are not provided in the chat history" when the dates were in fact present — i.e., noisy context induced false doubt [^23]. Family styles diverge: Claude abstains under ambiguity (lowest hallucination), GPT "show[s] the highest rates of hallucination, often generating confident but incorrect responses when distractors are present" [^23].

**Multi-turn decay [measured].** "LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation" (ICLR 2026): across 200k+ simulated conversations, all tested top models dropped **39% on average** vs. single-turn across six generation tasks — decomposed as "a minor loss in aptitude and a significant increase in unreliability"; "when LLMs take a wrong turn in a conversation, they get lost and do not recover," and models "prematurely attempt to generate final solutions, on which they overly rely" [^22]. For a chat+tool harness this is the single most relevant external number: it is the cost of unmanaged transcript growth and stale hypotheses.

**Deployer controls (official mechanisms) [documented].**
- **OpenAI compaction:** server-side `context_management: [{type:"compaction", compact_threshold: N}]` (docs example uses 200,000) triggers compaction when the rendered count crosses the threshold and emits an opaque compaction item that carries state forward "using fewer tokens"; or call standalone `/responses/compact` and pass its window as canonical (don't prune it). Latency tip: after appending outputs you may drop items preceding the latest compaction item [^4].
- **Anthropic context editing:** `clear_tool_uses_20250919` clears oldest tool results first when context exceeds `trigger` (default 100,000 input tokens), keeping the last `keep` (default 3) tool uses, with `clear_at_least` (so clearing is worth the cache break), `exclude_tools` (protect critical outputs), `clear_tool_inputs`. The framing is the mechanism claim: "context is a finite resource with diminishing returns, and irrelevant content degrades model focus" [^10] — consistent with [^21][^23]. There's also `clear_thinking_20251015` for thinking blocks and an SDK compaction mode whose summary schema (task overview / current state / discoveries / next steps / context to preserve) is worth mirroring in any hand-rolled compaction [^10].
- **State round-trip hygiene:** pass reasoning items back with function outputs (OpenAI — better results, fewer tokens; truncating is allowed but "keep everything between the last user message and your function call output… untouched") [^1][^3]; return Claude thinking blocks complete and unmodified in tool loops (required for reasoning continuity) and build `messages` append-only — client-side edits to earlier turns can invalidate later thinking blocks' signatures, and accounts created on/after 2026-08-31 get rejected for replaying invalidated blocks [^7][^10]; return Gemini `thought_signatures` unchanged [^14].

**[analysis]** Ranked mechanisms for a coding agent: (1) trim/clear stale tool output (biggest: distractor harm compounds with length [^23][^21]); (2) compaction on a threshold (bounds multi-turn unreliability growth [^22][^4][^10]); (3) put fresh task state/next steps at the *end* of context (primacy/recency [^20]); (4) reasoning-state round-trip (efficiency + continuity, unquantified [^1][^7]); (5) `reasoning.context: current_turn` on very long OpenAI runs to shrink rendered context [^1].

### 7. What the knobs do about second-guessing specifically **[analysis]**

Three measured mechanisms connect runtime knobs to the doubt-spiral problem: (a) **budget overshoot → self-reversal**: extended reasoning is associated with abandoning previously correct answers [^18], and long traces are dominated by redundant-verification and self-contradiction loops (o-series/GPT-5 self-contradiction 31–34% of long traces) [^19] — capping effort (and using `task_budget`) removes the runway for spirals without touching prompts; (b) **context noise → false doubt**: Claude abstains on answers literally present in cluttered contexts [^23], so context hygiene is an anti-second-guessing lever; (c) **truncation → hedging/aborted answers**: starved caps produce empty or mid-think failures [^1][^19], which look like indecision to users. The counterweight evidence matters: aggressive "be concise / stop deliberating" prompting does cut tokens but costs accuracy [^19], and Anthropic warns suppressing thinking lowers quality on reasoning-heavy tasks [^13]. **Recommendation to the prompt-text owner:** pair any "don't second-guess settled answers" clause with *effort ceilings per step type* rather than global think-less instructions, and use the officially sanctioned steering phrases (`"Answer directly without deliberating."` on routine confirmations; `"Please think hard before responding."` on planning/debugging) appended per message, not global suppression [^9].

## Caveats, limitations & open questions

- **Scope boundary:** multi-pass scaffolds (self-consistency, judge models), model switching, and pricing tables belong to teammates; the tool-execution-verification question is adjacent here — the one relevant datum: giving reasoning models tools (calculator/REPL/executor) raised accuracy +1.9 to +14.8 pts on easy tasks and +20–35 pts on hard algorithmic tasks at base size, but did not remove verbosity or scale-with-size collapse [^19] **[measured, basic-math/algorithmic tasks]**.
- **Generalization risk:** the sharpest "more thinking hurts" numbers come from basic-math benchmarks [^19] and constructed tasks [^17][^18], plus one practitioner refactor suite [^26]. Coding agents with test feedback may behave better (execution gives ground truth). Direction of the effect (diminishing returns past medium; over-engineering at high on bounded code) is corroborated across ≥3 independent sources; exact point sizes are workload-specific — **measure your own effort sweep** (all three providers say this explicitly [^1][^8][^14]).
- **Unverified secondary claims (not cited as fact):** that Gemini 3.6 Flash/3.5 Flash-Lite *silently accept-but-ignore* `temperature`/`top_p`/`top_k`, and that future generations will return HTTP 400 — reported by third-party blogs in Jul 2026 but not confirmed in fetched official docs (official sources confirm only deprecation + "strip" instruction [^15][^16]). Also unverified: per-model GPT-5.x default efforts beyond those stated in [^1] (community posts say `medium` for gpt-5; I did not fetch a confirming source).
- **Provider claims without published data:** "adaptive thinking reliably drives better performance than fixed `budget_tokens`" [^13]; reasoning-item round-trip efficiency gains [^1]; thinking-block preservation has "no intelligence impact" [^7]; `task_budget` graceful-finish behavior [^11]. Treat as plausible, not measured.
- **Conflicts surfaced, not blended:** (a) position effects: strong U-curve in [^20] vs. none in [^23]'s NIAH variant — task- and model-dependent; (b) GPT-6 sampling parameters: Azure's table ✅ vs. OpenAI's "remove when effort ≠ none" — reconciled as effort-conditional [^5][^2]; (c) LLMThinkBench effort plateaus [^19] vs. Artificial Analysis large low-end intelligence deltas [^25] — reconciled by task difficulty (easy tasks plateau; hard mixed suites don't).
- **Recency caveat:** LLMThinkBench's GPT-5/o3/Gemini-2.5 numbers are on older model generations than GPT-6/Opus 5/3.8 Flash; the *pattern* (plateau above medium on easy tasks) is what transfers, not the exact percentages. Anthropic's `task_budget` and per-message effort are **beta** — behavior may change.
- **Open question:** no fetched study isolates "runtime knobs' effect on second-guessing/self-reversal rates" directly; the link to [^18][^19][^23] is mechanistic inference **[analysis]**. A harness-side A/B (effort ceiling vs. no ceiling, tracking answer-flip rate and re-read-then-retract events) would settle it.
